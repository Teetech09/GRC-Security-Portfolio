# SecureTrust Microfinance Bank – Acceptable Use Policy

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates the development of an Acceptable Use Policy for
**SecureTrust Microfinance Bank Ltd.**, a fictional Nigerian microfinance
institution.

The project uses the **SANS Acceptable Use Standard (April 2025)** as the
primary baseline and adapts its requirements to a simulated financial-services
environment.

The objective was not simply to reproduce a policy template, but to review,
customize, and document security requirements based on the organization's
information assets, users, business activities, and security risks.

---

## Business Scenario

SecureTrust Microfinance Bank provides financial services to individuals
and small businesses.

For this simulation, the organization processes and stores sensitive
information including:

- Customer personal information
- Financial information
- Transaction records
- Employee information
- Authentication credentials
- Internal business information

The bank also relies on employees, contractors, banking applications,
databases, networks, endpoints, email systems, cloud services, and
third-party services to support its operations.

Inappropriate use of these resources could expose SecureTrust to risks such
as unauthorized access, data breaches, fraud, phishing, malware,
ransomware, insider misuse, and financial loss.

---

## Project Objective

The objective of this project was to develop a practical Acceptable Use
Policy that:

- Defines appropriate use of SecureTrust technology resources
- Protects sensitive customer and financial information
- Establishes user security responsibilities
- Defines prohibited activities
- Reduces risks associated with unauthorized access and misuse
- Establishes requirements for policy exceptions and sanctions
- Defines policy ownership and review requirements

---

## Methodology

The project followed a simple policy-development process:

1. Reviewed the SANS Acceptable Use Standard.
2. Defined a fictional microfinance bank business scenario.
3. Identified the users, information, systems, and technology resources
   within scope.
4. Identified security risks relevant to the scenario.
5. Reviewed each section of the SANS baseline for applicability.
6. Customized relevant requirements for the SecureTrust environment.
7. Added banking-specific requirements where appropriate.
8. Added document control, exception management, and policy review
   requirements.
9. Documented the major customization decisions and their security
   rationale.

The policy was developed with consideration for risks including:

- Insider misuse

- Phishing and social engineering
- Business Email Compromise (BEC)
- Fraud
- Malware and ransomware
- Unauthorized software
- Accidental information disclosure
- Unauthorized security testing
- Social media data exposure
- Loss of user accountability

- Social media and public communications

---

## Key Customizations

Several requirements were adapted specifically for the SecureTrust scenario.

Examples include:

- Restricting access to customer information to legitimate business purposes
- Prohibiting use of customer information for personal interest or financial gain
- Protecting passwords, authentication codes, and tokens
- Restricting personal email and unauthorized cloud services
- Requiring verification of unusual financial requests
- Addressing phishing and Business Email Compromise
- Preventing accidental disclosure through workplace photographs and screenshots
- Establishing formal policy exception and review requirements

Detailed reasoning for these decisions is documented in the
`Policy_Customization_Notes.md` file.

## Key Learning

One of the key lessons from this project was the distinction between
**authorized access and authorized use**.

An employee may have technical access to a system because of their role,
but that does not automatically authorize them to access every record
available through that system.

Access to sensitive customer or financial information must have a
legitimate and authorized business purpose.

The project also reinforced that security policy templates should be used
as baselines rather than copied without considering the organization's
specific business environment and risks.
