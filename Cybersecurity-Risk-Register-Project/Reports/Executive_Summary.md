Organisation: NorthBridge Financial Services Ltd.  
Assessment Type: Simulated Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

1. Overview

A simulated cybersecurity risk assessment was conducted for NorthBridge Financial Services Ltd., a fictional Canadian financial services organisation.

The assessment focused on identifying potential cybersecurity risks affecting the organisation's information assets, digital banking services, cloud infrastructure and third-party relationships.

Eight cybersecurity risks were identified and evaluated using a qualitative 5 × 5 risk assessment methodology.

The results indicate that all identified risks require management attention.

3. Key Areas of Concern

The assessment identified five particularly important areas requiring attention:

1. Identity and Access Management

Former employees may retain active system accounts if offboarding procedures are not properly enforced.

2. Cloud Security and Customer Data Protection

Misconfigured AWS S3 permissions could expose sensitive customer information to unauthorised users.

3. Ransomware Preparedness

Insufficiently tested incident response and recovery arrangements could increase the impact of a ransomware attack.

4. Third-Party Security

Inadequate vendor security assessments could expose the organisation to risks arising from payment processing partners.

5. Backup and Recovery

Unverified backup restoration capabilities could prolong business disruption following a cyber incident.

4. Recommended Management Actions

Management should prioritise the following actions:

- Strengthen employee offboarding and account deactivation procedures.
- Enforce appropriate AWS S3 security configurations.
- Improve phishing awareness and employee security training.
- Establish vulnerability remediation deadlines.
- Conduct ransomware response exercises.
- Strengthen third-party security assessments.
- Perform regular backup restoration tests.
- Improve security monitoring and incident escalation.

Each action should have an accountable owner, target completion date and supporting remediation evidence.

5. Management Considerations

Management should:

1. Review and approve appropriate risk treatment plans.
2. Assign responsibility to designated risk owners.
3. Allocate resources for priority remediation activities.
4. Establish regular risk review and reporting processes.
5. Require evidence before remediation activities are marked complete.
6. Review residual risks and formally approve any risk acceptance.

6. Conclusion

The simulated assessment highlights the importance of proactive cybersecurity risk management in protecting financial services, customer information and business operations.

Management attention should initially focus on the Critical identity and access management risk, followed by the seven High risks.

Implementing the recommended improvements and monitoring their effectiveness will support stronger security governance and operational resilience.
