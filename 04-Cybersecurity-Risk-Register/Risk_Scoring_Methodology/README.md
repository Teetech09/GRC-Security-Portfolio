Risk Scoring Methodology

1. Purpose

This document defines the methodology used to identify, evaluate and classify cybersecurity risks within the simulated assessment of NorthBridge Financial Services Ltd.

It establishes a consistent approach for evaluating risk likelihood, potential business impact, inherent risk and residual risk.

2. Risk Assessment Method

The project uses a qualitative risk assessment approach based on a 5 × 5 risk matrix.

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Each risk receives:
- A likelihood score from 1 to 5.
- An impact score from 1 to 5.
- A calculated risk score between 1 and 25.

3. Likelihood Rating

| Score | Rating | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | The event is highly unlikely to occur. |
| 2 | Unlikely | The event could occur but is not expected under normal circumstances. |
| 3 | Possible | The event may occur under certain circumstances. |
| 4 | Likely | The event is expected to occur in many circumstances. |
| 5 | Almost Certain | The event is expected to occur frequently or is already recurring. |

4. Impact Rating

| Score | Rating | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Negligible | Minimal disruption with no significant business impact. |
| 2 | Minor | Limited disruption with manageable consequences. |
| 3 | Moderate | Noticeable operational or financial impact requiring management attention. |
| 4 | Major | Significant financial, operational, regulatory or reputational consequences. |
| 5 | Severe | Critical business disruption, substantial financial loss, major data exposure or serious regulatory consequences. |

5. Risk Rating Classification

The calculated risk score determines the risk category.

| Risk Score | Risk Rating | Required Attention |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Routine monitoring |
| 5–9 | Medium | Planned risk management |
| 10–16 | High | Priority remediation and management oversight |
| 17–25 | Critical | Urgent management attention and treatment |

These thresholds are project-specific and illustrative. Actual organisations should approve risk appetite and rating thresholds according to their business context.

6. Inherent Risk

Inherent risk is the level of risk before considering the effect of existing security controls.

Inherent Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

It represents the potential exposure associated with a threat exploiting a vulnerability and affecting an organisational asset.

7. Residual Risk

Residual risk is the level of risk remaining after considering existing security controls.

Residual risk must be assessed based on the actual design and effectiveness of existing controls.

Proposed remediation actions must not automatically reduce a residual risk score. A reduction should only be recorded when there is sufficient evidence that the relevant controls have been implemented and are operating effectively.

8. Risk Treatment Options

The project recognises four common risk treatment strategies:

| Treatment | Description |
|---|---|
| Mitigate | Introduce or improve controls to reduce likelihood or impact. |
| Avoid | Stop the activity that creates the risk. |
| Transfer | Share or transfer some financial or operational consequences through contractual or insurance arrangements. |
| Accept | Formally acknowledge and approve the risk within the organisation's risk appetite. |

Risk acceptance should be approved by an authorised risk owner or management representative.

9. Risk Assessment Considerations

The assessment considers:

- Criticality and sensitivity of affected assets.
- Potential financial and operational consequences.
- Confidentiality, integrity and availability impacts.
- Existing security controls.
- Regulatory and contractual obligations.
- Potential reputational damage.
- Effectiveness of proposed risk treatments.

10. Methodology Disclaimer

This methodology was developed for a simulated cybersecurity GRC portfolio project.

The scoring model, rating thresholds and risk descriptions are illustrative and are not presented as a formally approved methodology of a real organisation.
