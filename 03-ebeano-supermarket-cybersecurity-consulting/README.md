Project Overview

As part of my cybersecurity consulting portfolio, I developed a practical security strategy for Ebeano Supermarket, a simulated retail business operating multiple outlets in Africa.

The business relies on cloud-based Point-of-Sale (POS) systems, employee tablets, kitchen terminals, inventory systems, customer Wi-Fi, and social media platforms.

These technologies support daily operations but also introduce cybersecurity risks, particularly when employee access, network security, payment devices, and physical assets are not adequately protected.

The original educational scenario involved eight employees. I expanded the workforce to 28 employees to demonstrate more comprehensive access governance, segregation of duties, and internal security controls.

My objective was to develop affordable, risk-based recommendations that management could realistically consider for implementation.

Business Security Challenges

The scenario presented several important security concerns:

- Shared and weak employee passwords.
- Limited access restrictions across business systems.
- Free customer Wi-Fi potentially exposing internal networks.
- Unattended staff tablets and workstations.
- Phishing and social engineering attacks.
- Unauthorized POS device substitution and payment diversion.
- Ransomware and operational disruption.
- Inadequate segregation of duties and employee accountability.

These risks could affect financial transactions, customer confidence, operational continuity, and business reputation.

Project Objectives

The project aimed to:

1. Strengthen employee identity and access management.
2. Introduce role-based permissions and least privilege.
3. Protect POS devices and payment transactions.
4. Separate customer Wi-Fi from operational networks.
5. Improve employee awareness of phishing and social engineering.
6. Strengthen physical security and asset accountability.
7. Identify and prioritize the supermarket's top three cybersecurity risks.
8. Recommend affordable controls and a practical implementation roadmap.

 My Consulting Approach

I approached the project from a governance, risk, and compliance perspective.

My process involved:

1. Understanding the supermarket's business operations and technology dependencies.
2. Identifying important business assets and potential threats.
3. Assessing access control and operational security weaknesses.
4. Designing preventive, detective, and administrative controls.
5. Prioritizing risks based on likelihood and business impact.
6. Recommending practical mitigation measures.
7. Developing a phased security implementation roadmap.

The project combines cybersecurity governance with technical, operational, and physical security considerations.



Project Deliverables

 1. Identity and Access Management (IAM) Policy

Developed an IAM policy for the expanded 28-employee workforce.

Key recommendations include:

- Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
- Principle of Least Privilege.
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Segregation of Duties (SoD).
- Dual Control / Four-Eyes Principle.
- Job Rotation and Mandatory Leave.
- Employee onboarding and offboarding.
- Privileged access management.
- POS transaction authorization and monitoring.

[View IAM Policy](./iam-policy/README.md)

 2. Network Security Plan

Designed a proposed segmented network architecture to protect business systems.

The network design includes:

- Guest Wi-Fi — VLAN 10.
- POS and Payment Network — VLAN 20.
- Staff Operations Network — VLAN 30.
- Administration Network — VLAN 40.
- IT Management Network — VLAN 50.

Additional recommendations cover firewall rules, secure wireless configurations, ransomware risk reduction, and restricted administrative access.

A proposed network architecture diagram is included in the documentation.

[View Network Security Plan](./network-security/README.md)

 3. Social Engineering Defense and Staff Awareness

Developed three fictional phishing scenarios targeting different employee roles:

1. A fake supplier invoice targeting the Store Manager.
2. A fraudulent brand collaboration targeting social media employees.
3. A fake IT support message targeting floor sales representatives.

I also prepared a five-minute staff security awareness training script covering phishing detection, credential protection, device security, and incident reporting.

 4. Physical Security and Asset Protection

Developed physical security recommendations addressing:

- Unattended employee tablets.
- Unauthorized POS device substitution.
- POS asset registration and verification.
- CCTV and surveillance.
- Restricted-area access.
- Visitor and contractor management.
- Device custody and accountability.
- Physical security incident response.

The plan also integrates dual control, job rotation, and mandatory leave with operational security practices.

[View Physical Security Plan](./physical-security/README.md)

 5. Cybersecurity Risk Assessment and Mitigation

Conducted a qualitative, scenario-based risk assessment using a 5 × 5 likelihood and impact matrix.

The three priority risks identified were:

| Risk | Inherent Score | Rating |
|---|---:|---|
| POS Payment Fraud and Device Substitution | 20/25 | Critical |
| Phishing and Credential Compromise | 16/25 | High |
| Ransomware and Poor Network Segmentation | 15/25 | High |

For each risk, I documented:

- Risk description and business impact.
- Likelihood and impact scores.
- Recommended mitigation controls.
- Proposed residual risk.
- Risk ownership.
- Affordable implementation options.

The assessment also includes a 30-, 60-, and 90-day security improvement roadmap.

Key Recommendations to Management

The most important recommendations are:

1. Protect payment revenue. 
Introduce POS verification, dual approval for high-risk changes, and daily payment reconciliation.

2. Strengthen employee access security.  
Use individual accounts, MFA, RBAC, and regular access reviews.

3. Isolate critical networks.
Separate customer Wi-Fi from POS, staff, and administrative systems.

4. Build employee security awareness.  
Provide regular phishing awareness training and establish clear reporting procedures.

5. Strengthen physical security.
Secure tablets, POS devices, network equipment, and restricted areas.

6. Review and monitor risks.  
Maintain risk, access, asset, and incident registers, and review security controls regularly.

Proposed Implementation Roadmap

| Timeline | Priority Activities |
|---|---|
| First 30 Days | POS verification, MFA, dual control, guest Wi-Fi isolation, staff awareness |
| Days 31–60 | Access reviews, firewall restrictions, device security, payment reconciliation, backups |
| Days 61–90 | Recovery testing, incident reporting, job rotation, security reviews, risk reassessment |

This phased approach prioritizes affordable controls that address the greatest business risks.
