1. Executive Summary

Ebeano Supermarket relies on employees to process customer payments, manage stock, prepare orders, maintain social media accounts, and operate business technology.

Although technical security controls are important, attackers may attempt to manipulate employees into revealing passwords, approving fraudulent payments, installing malicious software, or granting unauthorized access.

This proposed Social Engineering Defense Plan focuses on three employee groups:

1. Store managers and supervisors.
2. Social media and marketing employees.
3. Floor sales representatives.

The plan includes realistic phishing scenarios, warning signs, response procedures, and a five-minute security awareness training script.

All scenarios are fictional and intended for defensive education.

 2. Objectives

The objectives are to:

- Improve employees' ability to recognize phishing and social engineering.
- Reduce the risk of fraudulent payments and credential theft.
- Protect business social media accounts.
- Prevent unauthorized access to POS and staff devices.
- Encourage employees to verify unusual requests.
- Establish a simple reporting process.
- Build a security-conscious workplace culture.

3. Common Social Engineering Threats

| Threat | Description | Potential Business Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing | Fraudulent messages designed to steal information or trigger unsafe actions | Account compromise |
| Spear Phishing | Targeted phishing against a specific employee or role | Financial fraud |
| Business Email Compromise | Impersonation of a supplier, manager, or business partner | Payment diversion |
| Smishing | Phishing delivered through SMS | Credential theft |
| Vishing | Fraudulent phone calls requesting information or access | Unauthorized disclosure |
| Pretexting | Using a fabricated story to gain trust | Unauthorized access |
| MFA Fatigue | Repeated or deceptive authentication requests | Account takeover |
| QR Code Phishing | Malicious QR codes directing users to fraudulent websites | Credential theft or malware |

4. Scenario One: Fake Supplier Invoice Targeting the Store Manager

 Scenario

The Store Manager receives an email that appears to come from a familiar supermarket supplier.

The message claims an outstanding invoice must be paid immediately to prevent delivery disruption.

It includes an invoice attachment and requests payment to a newly provided bank account.

 Fictional Training Example

From: Accounts Department — Example Food Supplies  
Subject: Urgent: Outstanding Invoice and Updated Payment Details

> Dear Store Manager,
>
> Our records show that your recent supply invoice remains unpaid.
>
> To avoid interruption of tomorrow's delivery, please review the attached invoice and process payment using our updated bank details today.
>
> Kindly treat this as urgent.
>
> Accounts Department

*This is a fictional training example, not an actual supplier communication.*

Warning Signs

- Unexpected urgency.
- Unverified changes to bank details.
- Pressure to bypass approval procedures.
- An unexpected attachment.
- A sender address that may not match the supplier's verified contact details.

Recommended Employee Response

1. Do not immediately open unexpected attachments.
2. Check the sender's complete email address.
3. Contact the supplier using a previously verified phone number.
4. Confirm any change to bank details through an independent channel.
5. Apply dual control before approving payment changes.
6. Report suspicious messages to the manager or IT administrator.

Security Controls

- Supplier verification procedures.
- Dual approval for payment detail changes.
- MFA for business email accounts.
- Email filtering and anti-phishing protection.
- Staff awareness training.
- Independent payment reconciliation.

Potential Business Impact

A successful attack could lead to fraudulent supplier payments, financial losses, disruption of stock deliveries, and reputational damage.



5. Scenario Two: Fake Brand Collaboration Targeting Social Media Staff

Scenario

A social media employee receives a direct message from an account claiming to represent a popular consumer brand.

The sender offers a promotional partnership and asks the employee to open a link to review campaign materials.

The link leads to a fake social media login page designed to steal account credentials.

Fictional Training Example

Platform: Social Media Direct Message  
Sender: Brand Partnership Team

> Hello Ebeano Team!
>
> We love your supermarket content and would like to invite you to participate in our upcoming promotional campaign.
>
> Please review the collaboration details and confirm your business account through our partnership portal.
>
> We look forward to working with you!

*The fictional message represents a credential-harvesting attempt.*

 Warning Signs

- Unsolicited partnership offers.
- Requests to log in through unfamiliar links.
- Pressure to verify an account immediately.
- Unusual spelling in the sender's account name or website.
- Requests for passwords, recovery codes, or MFA codes.

Recommended Employee Response

1. Do not enter credentials through a link in an unsolicited message.
2. Verify the brand through its official website and established contact channels.
3. Open the social media platform directly rather than following the supplied link.
4. Never share passwords or MFA recovery codes.
5. Report suspicious partnership messages.
6. Escalate genuine collaboration requests for management approval.

Security Controls

- MFA or passkeys on social media accounts.
- Role-based access for marketing staff.
- Restricted administrative permissions.
- Approved password manager.
- Regular access reviews.
- A formal process for verifying business partnerships.

Potential Business Impact

A compromised social media account could be used to publish fraudulent promotions, scam customers, damage brand reputation, or lock the business out of its accounts.


 6. Scenario Three: Fake IT Support Message Targeting Floor Sales Representatives

Scenario

A floor sales representative receives a message claiming to come from the supermarket's IT administrator.

The message states that the employee's POS account will be suspended unless they immediately complete a security update.

It asks the employee to open a link and enter their login credentials.

Fictional Training Example

Sender: IT Support Desk  
Subject: Immediate POS Account Verification Required

> Attention Staff,
>
> We are updating the supermarket's POS security system.
>
> Employees who do not verify their accounts today may temporarily lose access to the sales platform.
>
> Please complete the verification process using the provided link.
>
> IT Support Team

*This is a simulated phishing message for awareness training.*

Warning Signs

- Unexpected requests for login credentials.
- Threats of immediate account suspension.
- Unverified links.
- Messages that create fear or urgency.
- Instructions that do not follow established IT procedures.

Recommended Employee Response

1. Do not click the suspicious link.
2. Do not provide passwords or MFA codes.
3. Contact the IT administrator using an established internal contact method.
4. Report the message to a supervisor.
5. If credentials were entered, notify IT immediately and follow account recovery procedures.

Security Controls

- Individual POS accounts.
- Least privilege.
- MFA where supported.
- Staff awareness training.
- Approved IT communication channels.
- Prompt incident reporting.

Potential Business Impact

A successful attack could expose employee credentials, allow unauthorized POS access, disrupt sales, or support fraudulent transactions.

7. Five-Minute Staff Security Awareness Training Script

Training Title: Think Before You Click — Protecting Ebeano Supermarket

Audience: All 28 Employees

Duration: Approximately 5 Minutes

Training Format: Short team briefing or staff meeting

Minute 1: Introduction

Good morning, everyone.

Today, we are discussing how to protect Ebeano Supermarket from cybercriminals who use fake emails, text messages, social media messages, and phone calls to trick employees.

Cybersecurity is not only the responsibility of the IT administrator.

Whether you work as a cashier, chef, sales representative, supervisor, inventory officer, or social media employee, your actions can help protect our customers and business.

 Minute 2: Recognizing Suspicious Messages

Imagine receiving a message saying your POS account will be blocked unless you click a link immediately.

Or imagine receiving an email from a supplier asking you to transfer money to a new bank account.

These messages may look genuine, but criminals often use urgency, fear, and attractive offers to make people act without checking.

Before responding, ask yourself:

- Was I expecting this message?
- Do I recognize the sender?
- Is the request unusual?
- Am I being pressured to act quickly?
- Can I verify the request independently?

Minute 3: Protecting Accounts and Devices

Never share your password, MFA code, or recovery code with another person.

Use your assigned account rather than another employee's login.

Lock your tablet or workstation when stepping away.

Do not allow unauthorized individuals to operate company POS devices.

If you notice a different POS terminal at your workstation, report it before processing payments.

These simple habits help protect our business.

Minute 4: What to Do When You Suspect an Attack

If you receive a suspicious message, do not click the link or open an unexpected attachment.

Do not reply with confidential information.

Contact the sender through a trusted phone number or established communication channel.

Report the incident to your supervisor or IT administrator.

If you have already clicked a link or entered your password, report it immediately.

Early reporting can prevent a small mistake from becoming a major security incident.

Minute 5: Conclusion and Staff Commitment

Remember these five rules:

1. Stop and think before clicking.
2. Verify unusual requests independently.
3. Never share passwords or MFA codes.
4. Lock and protect company devices.
5. Report suspicious activity immediately.

Protecting Ebeano Supermarket is everyone's responsibility.

Thank you for helping keep our customers, colleagues, and business secure.



8. Employee Reporting Procedure

Employees should use the following process:

1. Stop interacting with the suspicious message.
2. Avoid deleting relevant evidence before reporting.
3. Notify the IT administrator or designated supervisor using an established communication channel.
4. Provide the sender's details, message time, and a screenshot if safe and permitted.
5. Explain whether any links were opened, information was shared, or payments were made.
6. Follow instructions for account recovery or device isolation.
7. Do not forward suspicious attachments to colleagues.

Employees should be encouraged to report mistakes promptly without fear of automatic punishment.

The supermarket should track:

- Percentage of employees completing training.
- Number of suspicious messages reported.
- Time taken to report suspected incidents.
- Results of approved phishing simulations.
- Reduction in repeated unsafe behaviors.
- Employee understanding through short quizzes.

Training results should be used to improve awareness, not publicly embarrass employees.
